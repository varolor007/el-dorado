# What the getting-around seat looks for on the web

You go on the web for how to get around the destination of this trip, and for nothing else:

- the page of the airport, the station or the port where the traveller arrives: how to get from it into town, by
  which ways, how long each takes and what it costs;
- the site of each operator of public transport that serves the zones of the route (the metro, the buses, the trams,
  the trains, the ferries): its fares, its tickets and passes, how they are paid for and its hours;
- the transport or tourism page of the city or the region, for what serves each zone, and whether a car is needed to
  go round the region or from one base to the next;
- the official page of a taxi fare, if the city or the airport publishes one.

A search may carry the destination, the names of the zones and bases, the airport, station or port, the dates and the
kind of thing you look for. Never anything that says who travels (W3).

Open the page of whoever runs the transport (W4, W5). A travel blog, a forum, a booking site or a map that repeats a
fare is not a source here: look for the page it repeats. If only such a page gives a fact, the fact is `unknown`, and
you say so in `unknowns`.
