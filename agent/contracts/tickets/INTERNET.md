# What the tickets seat looks for on the web

You go on the web for the sights of `sights.json`, on the dates of this trip, and for nothing else:

- each sight's own site: its pages for visiting, tickets, prices, opening hours, closures and booking;
- if it has none, the official page of whoever runs it: a ministry, a city, a parks or heritage service;
- the official booking page the sight itself links to.

A search may carry the sight's name, the destination, the month and year of the trip and the kind of thing you look
for (tickets, hours, booking). Never a name of who travels, their ages written with anything that says who they are,
or anything else from `in/` beyond the sight and the place (W3).

Open the sight's page itself (W4, W5). A ticket reseller, a travel agency, a review site, a blog or a map service is
never a source here, even if it repeats the sight's prices: look for the page it repeats. The pages of
`open_data.json` are not sources of a price or an hour: what they say is already given, and the house keeps it.
