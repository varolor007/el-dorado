# What the local-knowledge seat looks for on the web

You go on the web for this destination and the zones of this route, and for nothing else:

- what each zone of the route is like (busy or quiet, by day and by night, what is there), from the official tourism
  page of the place, the city or the region;
- the hours of the place: when people eat lunch and dinner, what closes at midday or on a weekday, from the official
  tourism page or the page of whoever runs it;
- money: the currency, whether cards are taken, tipping, from an official tourism or government page;
- the practical things: the plug and the voltage, whether the tap water is drinkable, a phone card for visitors, the
  emergency numbers, from the authority that decides each one;
- what is better avoided (a scam, an area at night, a season, a habit that offends), from an official travel advice
  or tourism page;
- where to eat: the areas and the markets near each zone, the dishes of the place and what a meal usually costs,
  from the official tourism page or the page of the market itself. Never from a page of reviews or ratings.

A search may carry the destination, a zone's `label`, the month and year, the language and the kind of thing you look
for. Nothing else.

Prefer the page of whoever decides (W5): the tourism office, the market, the authority. A blog or a page that only
repeats another is weaker: say so in your notes, and look for the one that decides.
